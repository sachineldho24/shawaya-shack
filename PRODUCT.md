# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

People choosing a satisfying grilled meal for themselves, their family, or a group of friends. They are often browsing on a phone, hungry, and looking for a quick read on the food, portions, and ordering path without wading through restaurant marketing.

## Product Purpose

Present Shawaya Shack as a confident, authentic masala-shawaya restaurant and move visitors from appetite to a clear menu choice. Success means the food is understood within seconds, the brand feels memorable, and visitors can assemble and share an order request without confusion.

## Positioning

Traditional masala, slow fire, and generous plates made to share.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: choose a plate and build an order request.
- Secondary CTA: see how the shawaya is prepared.
- The line a visitor should remember after ten seconds: **Slow heat. Deep flavor.**
- Belief ladder: the food looks worth craving; the masala and grilling feel authentic; the portions fit solo and shared occasions; choosing a plate is easy.
- Proof on hand: the supplied Shawaya Shack logo, brand description, approved reference prices, and commissioned food imagery documented in `assets/ASSET_GUIDE.md`. No customer testimonials, ratings, addresses, hours, or ordering-provider details have been supplied, so the website must not invent them. Showcase prices should be confirmed before launch.

## Brand Personality

Fire-led, generous, and direct. The voice is short, sensory, and confident without unsupported superlatives. The experience should feel energetic enough for a social meal and considered enough to signal premium ingredients.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic fast-food red-and-yellow templates, rustic-western grill clichés, cartoon flames, glossy plastic surfaces, crowded card grids, decorative glassmorphism, continuous parallax, fabricated heritage, fake reviews, and order buttons that lead nowhere.

## Design Principles

1. Put real food texture before decoration.
2. Let one appetite-driving idea dominate each section.
3. Make the next useful action obvious on a phone.
4. Use motion to reveal hierarchy and acknowledge interaction, never to delay ordering.
5. Be transparent when real business details or an external ordering channel are not yet configured.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA fundamentals: readable contrast, semantic landmarks, keyboard access, visible focus, 44px touch targets, descriptive image alternatives, zoom-safe layouts, and a reduced-motion experience that preserves hierarchy without spatial travel.
